What is a sprint in data analysis?
A sprint is a short, focused period of work during which a team completes a specific task or part of a project.
Instead of trying to complete a whole data analysis project at once, the team breaks it into smaller tasks called sprints.
A sprint usually lasts 1-2 weeks, although the length can vary.
Think of it as completing your assignment in small stages, with a clear goal for each stage.
Example: Sweet Layers (your data analysis assignment)
Remember the Sweet Layers business? The owner says:
"Orders are increasing, but profits are not improving. Customers are not coming back. Where should I spend my marketing money?"
Imagine you are the data analyst working on this project.
You could divide the work into four sprints:
Sprint 1: Understand the problem
Week 1
* Understand the business problem.
* Identify the questions that need answering.
* Decide which data is required.
Deliverable: Data analysis plan
Sprint 2: Collect and clean data
Week 2
* Collect sales data.
* Collect customer data.
* Check for missing or duplicate records.
* Correct errors in the data.
Deliverable: Clean dataset
Sprint 3: Analyse the data
Week 3
* Calculate profit margins.
* Identify returning customers.
* Compare sales across products.
* Identify which marketing campaigns perform best.
Deliverable: Analysis and findings
Sprint 4: Present recommendations
Week 4
* Create charts and dashboards.
* Explain what the data reveals.
* Recommend where the business should invest its marketing budget.
Deliverable: Final report and recommendations
What happens at the end of a sprint?
At the end of each sprint, the team usually:
1. Reviews what has been completed.
2. Checks whether the sprint goal was achieved.
3. Discusses challenges.
4. Decides what needs to happen next.

What is a Daily Stand-up Meeting?
A daily stand-up is a short team meeting, usually lasting 10-15 minutes, where everyone shares their progress and explains what they are working on.
The purpose is to make sure everyone is moving towards the sprint goal and to identify problems early.
It is called a stand-up because traditionally people stand during the meeting to keep it short!
Example: Sweet_Layers data analysis project
Imagine you are part of a team analysing Sweet_Layers' sales and customer data.
Your team is in Sprint 2: Collecting and cleaning data.

1. What is Agile methodology?
Agile is a way of managing projects by breaking big projects into smaller pieces of work, completing them step by step, and regularly reviewing progress.
Instead of spending six months trying to complete an entire project before showing anyone the results, an Agile team works in smaller cycles.
These cycles are called sprints.
The team:
1. Plans what needs to be done.
2. Works on a small part of the project.
3. Reviews the results.
4. Gets feedback.
5. Improves the work and moves to the next sprint.
The idea is to be flexible and make improvements as you learn more.

4. Important Agile terminology
There are a few terms you should understand:
1. Product Backlog
A list of all the tasks or requirements that need to be completed.
Example:
* Calculate monthly revenue.
* Calculate gross profit.
* Identify returning customers.
* Compare marketing campaigns.
2. Sprint Planning
The team decides which tasks it will complete during the next sprint.
3. Daily Stand-up
A short daily meeting where team members discuss progress, today's work and obstacles.
4. Sprint Review
The team demonstrates what it has completed and receives feedback.
For example, you show the Sweet_Layers owner a chart showing that chocolate cakes generate more revenue than vanilla cakes.
5. Sprint Retrospective
The team discusses how it worked together and what it could do better next time.

1. What are user stories in data analysis?
A user story is a short description of what a person or business wants to achieve using data.
It explains:
* Who needs the information?
* What information do they need?
* Why do they need it?
In simple terms, a user story explains what the user wants to know and why that information is important.
User stories are commonly used in Agile and Scrum projects to help data analysts understand business needs before starting their analysis.

2. A simple example: Sweet_Layers ??
Remember our Sweet_Layers business example?
The owner says:
"Orders are increasing, but profit doesn't feel like it. Customers don't seem to come back. Where should I put next year's marketing money?"
We can turn this business problem into user stories.
User story 1: Understanding profit
As the business owner, I want to see the profit generated by each product so that I can identify which products make the most money.
User story 2: Understanding customers
As the marketing manager, I want to know how many customers make repeat purchases so that I can improve customer retention.
User story 3: Understanding marketing
As the marketing manager, I want to compare sales generated by different marketing campaigns so that I can invest in the campaigns that deliver the best results.
Notice that each story tells us what the person wants and why.

There is a simple formula used in Agile:
As a [type of user]
I want [something I need]
So that [the benefit or reason].
For example:
"As a supermarket manager, I want to identify which products sell most during weekends so that I can ensure sufficient stock is available."
5. User stories vs analytical questions
These two are related, but they are not exactly the same.
User storyAnalytical questionI want to understand repeat customers.What percentage of customers purchased more than once?I want to understand product profitability.Which products generated the highest gross profit?I want to evaluate marketing.Which campaign generated the highest return on investment?Think of it this way:
* User story = What does the business person need and why?
* Analytical question = What specific question must the data analyst answer?
* Metric = How will we measure the answer?
